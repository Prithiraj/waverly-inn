16 Bank Street · New York

Bank Street, West Village

A room with a memory.

The Waverly Inn is less a reinvention of old New York than a continuation of it: close quarters, fireplaces, a famous mural and a front door tucked beneath a townhouse stoop.

A short history

Bank Street, across the decades.

The building is older than the restaurant. The restaurant's documented life as Ye Waverly Inn & Garden begins in the 1920s.

1844

The townhouse

Independent reporting identifies the Bank Street building as a 19th-century townhouse dating to 1844.

1920s

Ye Waverly Inn & Garden

The space became a sit-down restaurant in the 1920s, beginning the restaurant lineage that survives in its name and setting.

2006

The present era

Graydon Carter and partners reopened the Waverly Inn, retaining the intimate townhouse character while establishing the modern restaurant.

Today

A classic establishment

The dining room, conservatory and garden remain the core of the experience, with classic American cooking and a no-photography policy indoors.

On the wall

The mural is part of the room's identity.

Edward Sorel's mural depicts Greenwich Village personalities and stretches through the candlelit dining room. Rather than turning that art into a digital gimmick, this site lets photography carry the atmosphere and keeps the interface quiet.

The atmosphere

Discretion is part of the experience.

The restaurant's current materials explicitly prohibit photography inside. The result is unusual in modern dining: a room designed to be experienced rather than documented.
